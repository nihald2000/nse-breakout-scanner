Paper Trade Journal

Log the option trades you would take from the Index Options page, using the real option premiums you see in your broker. Everything stays in this browser (local storage); nothing is sent anywhere.

Paper results are the only out-of-sample evidence you will have. 20–30 sessions is the minimum; 50+ is better. Log the signals you skipped too, otherwise the record only shows the trades you liked. This is a record of your own trades, not proof of an edge.

Add an entry

Option R = (exit − entry) ÷ (entry − planned stop premium). Leave the stop blank if you have none; the % result still counts. Leave the exit blank while a trade is open. For a skipped signal only the date, index, strategy and side are needed.

Automated chart alerts · index levels from Full Chart

Every new trigger seen while the Full Chart page is open is saved here automatically. On a later load, only the latest trigger for that session is backfilled, not every earlier attempt. Rows logged after the trigger are marked (backfilled); only rows marked without that suffix were logged while the setup was still fresh. These are underlying index levels, not option premiums, so they are kept separate from the paper option statistics above. The Full Chart page must be open to poll; no server-side alert is stored.

Backtest vs paper

Backtest index R and option R (Black-Scholes approximation, no IV change) come from the last backtest run in this browser on the Index Options page. Paper option R comes from your entries above. They are not the same thing: the backtest is a model over about 40 sessions, and a paper log of a few trades is mostly noise. Do not read a gap as proof either way; read it as a prompt to look at your trades one by one.

Candlestick backtest · 15-minute read, underlying moves

Replays the same CE/PE checks the Sector Scope page uses on closed 15-minute candles over the last month, one bar at a time with no look-ahead, and records what the underlying did over the next few candles. If one candle touches both the target and the stop the order is unknowable and the row says both — it is neither a win nor a loss. Underlying moves only: an option's premium will differ, and spread, costs and slippage are not modelled. One month is a small in-sample window; this checks the read, it does not prove an edge.
