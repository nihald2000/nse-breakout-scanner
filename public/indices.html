Index Options Rulebook

NIFTY, BANKNIFTY, FINNIFTY, MIDCPNIFTY and NIFTYNXT50, checked against fixed rule sets (ORB, Ichimoku + Fib, VWAP, expiry-day max pain). Buy CE or buy PE only; nothing here sells options.

These are rule checks on the index price. No option premium, spread or open-interest data is used (Yahoo has none; a broker adapter would be needed). The rules are not proven: the Backtest tab replays them on about 40 recent sessions, which is too few to establish an edge. This is a sanity check on behaviour, not proof. Treat BUY as "all the rules line up, now check the option chain yourself", and paper-trade first (see the Journal tab).

    Signal: BUY CE (bullish) or BUY PE (bearish) appears only when the setup has triggered on a finished candle, the trigger is no more than 2 bars old, every blocking rule passes and India VIX is not elevated. WAIT means a setup is forming; the line under it says what is needed. NO TRADE means a rule rules it out today. INFO rows are information only and never count as a signal. CLOSED shows the state at the end of the last session. Only finished candles are used, so a forming candle never triggers anything. "Actionable only" counts BUY rows and nothing else.

    Data-quality tags on every row: true VWAP volume exists so VWAP is real; proxy the line is only a session average; no volume the index feed has no volume, so volume filters are skipped (and say so in the checks); VIX ok / VIX blocked / VIX n/a the India VIX filter; chain ok / no chain option-chain open interest (needs a broker adapter). A skipped filter is always visible in the checks list (hover the check count) and in these tags.

    Stop, T1, T2 are levels on the index, not the option. An index stop does not give a fixed loss on the option: premium moves non-linearly, spreads widen and a stop can fill worse. Risk % is the index stop distance as a share of price. The multiples are starting points to test, not validated edges.

    • ORB breakout (Track 1) (5m): the opening range is the 9:15 to 9:30 high and low. A 5m close above the high plus a buffer of 10% of the range width (below the low for puts) is the trigger, with no retest. The breakout candle needs volume at least 1.5x the mean of up to 20 earlier bars of the session; with no index volume that filter is skipped and says so. Stop: the lowest low (highest high for puts) from the breakout candle to the latest bar, capped at 0.7% of price. Targets 2R and 3R. Trail (information only, not simulated): VWAP, or the 20 EMA when VWAP is only a proxy.
    • ORB retest (Track 2): the earlier retest logic, unchanged: break, a retest within 10% of the width that holds, then a close beyond the retest candle. A close back inside the range cancels it, with no second attempt. Targets 1R and 2R. It is evaluated only when Track 1 has not fired. Both tracks: skipped on chop (both edges broken), after 14:00, or with a stop over 0.7%. The opening-range width no longer blocks; it sets the size hint: full from 0.5x to 2x the recent typical width, half from 2x to 3x (counts as 0.5R in the backtest), skip beyond 3x or under 0.5x (no trade).
    • Ichimoku + Fib, intraday (15m): the trend filter is ADX(14) on 15m at least 20 with +DI above -DI (long) or -DI above +DI (short); if ADX is unavailable the 5m 20-EMA slope over 10 bars is used. The 1h cloud is shown for information only and never blocks. On 15m, the last confirmed swing low then high (3 bars each side) is the leg; price must retrace 38.2% to 61.8% of it (deeper is PULLBACK TOO DEEP), then a candle must dip into the zone, close back beyond the 38.2% level and, when volume exists, have volume at least the mean of the prior 3 bars. No entries before 10:00 or after the 14:30 candle. Stop is the retrace extreme (cap 1.0%) and the prior swing must be at least 1R away.
    • Ichimoku swing (Daily / 4h): the same logic one level up: Daily cloud and structure as the trend filter (cloud at least 0.25% wide), 4h pullback and trigger. Stop cap 4.0% (my assumption). It is shown here but not backtested, and it holds for days, so size it as a swing trade.
    • VWAP reclaim (5m, needs a true VWAP): at least 9 consecutive closes (45 minutes) on the trend side of VWAP, then 1 to 3 closes across it, then a close back with volume at least the mean of the prior 2 candles. Stop at the dip low, cap 0.5%, targets 2R and 3R. RSI(9) must be 38 to 58 at the dip low for calls (45 to 65 at the dip high for puts); skipped, with a note, if the dip is inside the first 15 bars.
    • VWAP pullback (5m, needs a true VWAP): price on the trend side with the last two 5m swing lows rising; a dip to within 0.1% of VWAP that holds after 3 of the previous 6 closes were clear of it; trigger a close beyond that candle. Skipped on 3 or more VWAP crossings, a stop over 0.5%, or after 14:30; starts 9:50. Every VWAP row carries a reliability tag (high, medium, low); low means NO TRADE.
    • Expiry max pain (NIFTY and BANKNIFTY, expiry days, 14:00 to 15:30): needs open interest from an option chain, which this app does not have. Without a broker adapter the row says NO TRADE and never guesses. With one: max pain is the strike where option writers lose least; a 5m close toward it while price is 0.5% to 1.5% away triggers; T1 is the max pain level.
    • India VIX filter: when India VIX is above its 60th percentile of the previous 60 sessions, every BUY is downgraded to WAIT ("IV elevated"): buying premium when implied volatility is high is a poor trade. If the VIX cannot be fetched the filter is skipped and shown as such.

    Expiry days are flagged, and ORB, Ichimoku and VWAP stay blocked on them. Expiry is taken as Tuesday: every Tuesday for NIFTY (weekly), the last Tuesday of the month for the others. This follows the schedule NSE moved to in September 2025, but it does not know exchange holidays that shift an expiry, so check the NSE calendar. Lot sizes change from time to time, so none are shown; check the current lot and margin with your broker. Strike steps used for the ATM strike (NIFTY 50, BANKNIFTY 100, FINNIFTY 50, MIDCPNIFTY 25, NIFTYNXT50 100) are typical values; confirm them in the option chain.

    Before any trade: look at the actual option's bid, ask, volume and open interest, and skip it if the spread is wide. Size so that a stop-out loses only a small, fixed share of your account (0.25% to 0.5% is a common simulation range). No averaging down, no revenge trades, a daily loss limit, and exit everything by 15:15. A long option can lose its whole premium. Log 20 to 30 paper-trading sessions in the Journal tab, including the signals you skip, before putting real money behind any of these rules.

    Results by strategy (all tested indices together)

    Read option R with care. Option R divides the premium change by the premium you would lose at the stop. When the index stop is very tight (some breakout candles close almost on their low), that loss is tiny, so one trade can show ±40R and drag the average. The premium % beside each R figure is not affected by this. Index R is measured on the index: +1R means the index moved one stop-distance your way. Option P&L columns are a Black-Scholes approximation, not real premiums: India VIX (previous close) stands in for ATM implied volatility, +2 vol points for the 1-ITM strike, 6.5% interest, flat smile, and the option is re-priced at the exit with the same volatility ("no IV change") and with volatility 10% lower ("-10% IV", a crude IV-crush case). Each shows the average in option R (premium change divided by the premium you would lose if the index reached the stop) with its 95% range, and the average % change in premium. Expiry-week gamma is not modelled; assignment and pin risk are not modelled; no bid/ask spread beyond the option-cost box. Half-size trades count 0.5R. "Blocked" counts BUY signals that were held back by the India VIX filter or the opening-range size rule: they are not trades. The range after each average is a 95% confidence range: if it includes zero, this test shows no evidence of an edge.

    How to read this, and what it cannot tell you

    • What is tested. The exact live rules, replayed candle by candle. At each step the engine sees only candles that had finished by then (checked by scrambling every unfinished candle and confirming the rows do not change). One trade per strategy per session: the first BUY. Entry is the close of the trigger candle. Inside a candle the stop is assumed to hit before the target. Everything is flat by 15:15. Expiry days are skipped, as on the live page. The India VIX filter uses the previous session's close against the 60 sessions before it. Ichimoku swing (Daily/4h) and expiry max pain are not backtested: they need history and open-interest data this source does not have.
    • The sample is small. Yahoo only provides about 60 days of 5-minute data, roughly 40 sessions per index. Five indices move together, so they are not five independent tests. With under about 30 trades per strategy the confidence range is so wide that almost any result is consistent with luck. Rows with fewer than 30 trades are flagged. A flat or negative result is a valid result.
    • Option P&L is a model. It captures the index move and time decay under a flat-volatility Black-Scholes world. It does not capture the real spread, skew, volatility changes, or how premiums behave in expiry week. Treat it as a better guide than index R, and still not as the truth.
    • Do not tune on these sessions. The parameters were fixed before testing. Changing them until this table looks good would only fit the noise in 40 sessions. The paper-trading sessions you log in the Journal are the only out-of-sample evidence you will have: 20 to 30 sessions is the minimum, 50 or more is better.
    • Hit rate versus the reward. With a 1R target, a strategy must hit more than about half the time just to cover costs; with a 2R target, more than about a third; with 3R, more than a quarter. Compare the hit rate with those break-evens, and look at the maximum drawdown in R against what you could actually tolerate.
    • What would be convincing. A few hundred trades per strategy over at least a year or two, covering trending, choppy and high-volatility periods, tested on real option premiums with costs. That needs intraday history longer than Yahoo gives, for example from a broker's historical data API (see lib/broker.js), and a train / test split. Treat this page as a sanity check on behaviour, not as proof.
