Options Skew Map

India only — every NSE F&O stock, NSE monthly expiry at least 21 days out. The map reads the live option chain and compares the 25Δ put IV with the 25Δ call IV, normalised by ATM IV. Positive means puts are richer than calls (downside demand); negative means calls are richer.

Descriptive positioning, not a forecast or a trade. IV is computed from NSE's quoted bid/ask mid with Black-Scholes (r=6.5%, no dividend), not from NSE's displayed IV. Thin or wide quotes are marked and are never zero-filled. The F&O ban list is a separate NSE publication and is not checked here. This is a screening aid, not trading advice.

How to read it: a 25Δ put is roughly the downside hedge strike; a 25Δ call is the upside strike. If puts are richer, the normalised skew is positive. The ATM IV is the average of the nearest-strike call and put IVs. A stock with no bracket, no ATM quote or no usable bid/ask is shown as n/a, not as zero. The Raw skew is in volatility points (a 3.2-point gap is not 3.2%). The Normalised column divides that gap by ATM IV, so a 3-point gap in a 15% IV name is a larger number than the same gap in a 40% IV name.
